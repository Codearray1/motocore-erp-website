Most two-wheeler showrooms in Nepal still run on a mix of registers, Excel sheets and WhatsApp. That works until the showroom gets busy: a follow-up is missed, two salespeople promise the same bike, a booking receipt cannot be found, or nobody knows which customers are still waiting for their namsari. Showroom software exists to fix exactly these gaps.

This checklist covers what a motorcycle, scooter or EV dealer should look for before choosing a system.

## 1. Inquiries and follow-ups that do not slip

Every walk-in and phone call is a possible sale. Good software records each inquiry with the model the customer is interested in, how serious they are (hot, warm or cold), where they heard about you and when they expect to buy. It should then tell your team who to call today and flag follow-ups that are overdue.

## 2. One customer, one record

Search by phone number before creating anything. If the same buyer is entered three times by three people, your reports and follow-ups fall apart. Look for a system that finds the existing customer first and links inquiries, bookings and payments to that one record.

## 3. Bookings with proper receipts

When a customer books a bike with an advance, they should get a numbered receipt, and the system should stop the same payment from being entered twice. The booking should hold the model, variant and colour so the right stock can be allocated later.

## 4. VIN-wise stock and allocation

Bikes are not interchangeable stock: each has its own VIN (chassis) and engine or motor number. Your software should record stock by VIN and only offer matching units (same model, variant and colour) when you allocate a bike to a paid booking.

## 5. Documents, delivery and namsari

Before handing over keys, you need a checklist of the customer's documents, a challan, and a delivery confirmation. After delivery, ownership transfer (namsari) can take time; tracking it until the bill book is handed over keeps customers from calling you to ask "what is the status?".

## 6. Money under control

Ask how the system handles:

- **Collections and receivables:** what customers, financiers and distributors still owe you, with daily collection entries.
- **Discounts with approval:** salespeople can request a discount with a reason, but only an owner or manager approves it.
- **Refunds and cancellations:** a refund should follow an approval and payout flow and never exceed what the customer paid.
- **Exchange vehicles:** recording an old bike's valuation and applying the exchange credit to the new deal.

Note that showroom software is not the same as statutory accounting software. Make sure you know which system your accountant uses for the books and VAT.

## 7. Roles and permissions

An owner, a sales manager, a finance manager and a sales officer need different access. The sales officer should not be able to approve their own discount or see every report.

## 8. A mobile app for the sales floor

Your team is not sitting at a desk. A mobile app lets them add inquiries and walk-in bookings from the floor, and lets the owner check bookings, payments and approvals from anywhere, ideally with push notifications.

## 9. Built for Nepal

Amounts in Nepalese rupees, the Nepali fiscal year, and support from a team in Nepal who understand how showrooms here actually work matter more than a long feature list.
